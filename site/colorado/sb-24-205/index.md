# What Happened to SB 24-205, Colorado's Original AI Act

> **Short answer.** SB 24-205 was signed May 17, 2024 as the first comprehensive state AI consumer protection law in the United States. It never took effect. Its February 1, 2026 start date was pushed to June 30, 2026; xAI sued to block it in April 2026; and on May 14, 2026 — six weeks before the delayed date — the legislature repealed and replaced the entire framework with [SB 26-189](/colorado/sb-26-189/). If you are compliance-planning against SB 24-205, you are planning against a dead statute.

If you searched "Colorado AI Act effective date" and got four different answers, this page is the reason. Guides written in 2024 and 2025 describe a law that no longer exists, using dates that were moved twice and a framework that was deleted.

---

## What SB 24-205 would have required

The original act was a **risk-management regime**. If you developed or deployed a high-risk AI system making consequential decisions about Coloradans, it would have imposed:

- A **duty of reasonable care** to protect consumers from algorithmic discrimination
- **Pre-deployment impact assessments**, updated annually
- A written **risk management program** and policy
- A **public statement** summarizing the high-risk systems you deployed
- **Reporting discovered algorithmic discrimination to the Attorney General within 90 days**
- AG enforcement as a deceptive trade practice, with civil penalties around $20,000 per violation, and a private right of action under existing discrimination statutes in some circumstances

That entire architecture was the most aggressive state AI framework in the country when it passed — and it is the reason so many national compliance guides still tell you to budget for "Colorado AI impact assessments." None of those duties ever applied to anyone.

## Timeline: signed, delayed, sued, replaced

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| May 17, 2024 | **SB 24-205 signed** — first comprehensive state AI law in the US. Effective date: February 1, 2026. |
| August 28, 2025 | SB 25B-004 delays the effective date to **June 30, 2026**. |
| April 9, 2026 | **xAI sues** the Attorney General (X. AI LLC v. Weiser, D. Colo. No. 1:26-cv-01515) — First Amendment, Equal Protection, Due Process, and Commerce Clause claims. |
| April 24, 2026 | The **United States intervenes** as plaintiff-intervenor — the first federal intervention in litigation against a state AI law. |
| April 27, 2026 | Court grants a **stipulated enforcement stay**: no enforcement or investigation under SB 24-205 or anything replacing it until 14 days after a ruling on xAI's preliminary-injunction motion. |
| **May 14, 2026** | **SB 26-189 signed.** SB 24-205 repealed and reenacted in full. New effective date: January 1, 2027. |
| June 30, 2026 | The delayed effective date **arrives for a law that no longer exists**. |

## What survived, what died

| SB 24-205 framework | Under SB 26-189 |
|---|---|
| Duty of reasonable care against algorithmic discrimination | **Removed** |
| Pre-deployment impact assessments | **Removed** |
| Annual impact assessment updates | **Removed** |
| Written risk management program | **Removed** |
| 90-day AG discrimination report | **Removed** |
| Public statement of high-risk systems | Replaced by developer-to-deployer documentation |
| Consumer notice before a consequential decision | **Kept and expanded** — plus a 30-day post-adverse-outcome disclosure |
| Right to appeal / human review | **Kept** — reframed as data access, correction, and meaningful human review |

The replacement shifted Colorado from a **governance model** (prove you assessed and managed the risk) to a **transparency model** (tell the consumer, and explain adverse outcomes). Full breakdown: [SB 26-189 explained](/colorado/sb-26-189/).

## The lawsuit outlived the law

xAI did not sue SB 24-205 into repeal — the legislature replaced it on its own timeline. But the case continues, and the April 27 stay was written to survive the replacement: it covers SB 24-205 **"or any legislation replacing or amending SB24-205 enacted during this legislative session."** SB 26-189 was enacted in that session, so the stay reaches it.

Three consequences worth knowing:

1. **Enforcement, not validity, is paused.** The law is on the books and its January 1, 2027 date is unchanged. The AG simply cannot initiate enforcement — or even an investigation — for violations occurring on or before 14 days after the court rules on xAI's preliminary-injunction motion.
2. **The clock starts with the final rules.** xAI must file its preliminary-injunction motion within 28 days after final adoption of the implementing rulemaking. The AG filed proposed rules August 11, 2026, released an interim draft October 6, and held the rulemaking hearing October 26. Until final rules land, the PI motion is not even due.
3. **Federal pressure runs in parallel.** On July 7, 2026, the FTC published a proposed policy statement suggesting that steering AI outputs to comply with a state law — it named Colorado's — may itself be deceptive under Section 5 of the FTC Act. It is not finalized and nothing has been ruled on, but it means Colorado's regime is contested from two directions at once.

## Why so many pages are still wrong

Three failure modes, in order of frequency:

**Frozen dates.** "Takes effect June 30, 2026" was true for eight months of 2025. It stopped being true on May 14, 2026, and the date passed into nothing. Pages that were accurate on publication are now describing a ghost.

**Zombie obligations.** Impact assessments, risk management programs, and the 90-day discrimination report are the most-cited features of the old law — and all five were deleted. A 2024-built compliance program does not need extending; it needs reorienting around disclosure.

**One name, two laws.** SB 26-189 kept the short title "Colorado AI Act." Search results for that phrase mix the two statutes freely. The quickest test for any guide: if it mentions an impact assessment or a 90-day report, it is describing SB 24-205, whatever it calls the law.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is SB 24-205 still law?**
No. It was repealed and reenacted in full by SB 26-189 on May 14, 2026. Nothing in SB 24-205 was ever operative — both of its effective dates passed after it had been repealed.

**What was the effective date of SB 24-205?**
Originally February 1, 2026, moved to June 30, 2026 by SB 25B-004 in August 2025. Both dates are historical trivia now; the operative date of the current law is January 1, 2027.

**What replaced it?**
SB 26-189, the Automated Decision-Making Technology Act. It keeps the "Colorado AI Act" short title but replaces risk-management duties with notice and disclosure duties. See [what actually is in effect](/colorado/) and [the full SB 26-189 breakdown](/colorado/sb-26-189/).

**Do I still need an impact assessment for Colorado?**
No. The mandatory pre-deployment impact assessment, annual updates, risk management program, and 90-day discrimination report were all removed. The compliance spine now is: pre-use notice, 30-day adverse-outcome disclosure, and meaningful human review.

**Can the Attorney General enforce the replacement law?**
Not yet. The April 27, 2026 stay in X. AI LLC v. Weiser pauses enforcement of SB 24-205 and its in-session replacement until 14 days after the court rules on xAI's preliminary-injunction motion — which itself is not due until 28 days after final rules are adopted. The law's January 1, 2027 date stands regardless.

**Did the xAI lawsuit kill the law?**
No. The legislature repealed and replaced it while the case was pending. The case continues against the replacement, but the stay is an enforcement pause, not a ruling on validity.
