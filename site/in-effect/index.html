Which State AI Disclosure Laws Are in Effect Right Now

Six states can enforce an AI disclosure or transparency duty against you today: California, Texas, Illinois, Utah, Maine, and New York. Colorado starts January 1, 2027. Two California election-AI laws — AB 2839 and AB 2655 — are barred by court order and cannot be enforced.

#What this table is not

It is not every AI law in the country. It covers disclosure and transparency obligations — the laws that require you to tell someone something — plus the prohibitions that travel with them. Bias-audit laws, procurement rules, and state-agency governance are tracked separately.

It also does not cover the roughly 26 states with narrow election deepfake statutes. Those bind campaigns rather than businesses and are handled in a separate section.

Frequently asked questions

How many states require AI disclosure?

Six states have enforceable disclosure or transparency duties as of November 2026. Colorado joins them on January 1, 2027. More than 26 additional states have narrow election-deepfake disclosure rules.

Is the Colorado AI Act in effect?

No. The 2024 law never became operative. The replacement, SB 26-189, starts January 1, 2027. → Full explanation

Can I rely on California's AB 2839 or AB 2655?

No. Both are barred by court order. Use Elections Code §20010 and AB 2355 instead. → What still applies

Do any of these require a bias audit?

Not among the disclosure laws. New York City's Local Law 144 requires one for automated employment decision tools; that is a city ordinance, not a state AI disclosure law.

What is the single strictest disclosure duty in the country right now?

For scope, Colorado from January 1, 2027 — every deployer, every covered domain, with a 30-day post-decision explanation.
