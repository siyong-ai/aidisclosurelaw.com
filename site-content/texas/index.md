# Texas AI Law: TRAIGA, in Force Since January 1, 2026

> **Short answer.** Texas's AI law is **TRAIGA**, the Texas Responsible Artificial Intelligence Governance Act (**HB 149**), signed June 22, 2025 and in force since **January 1, 2026**. The affirmative **disclosure** duty applies to exactly two groups: **government entities** interacting with consumers, and **health care providers** using AI in connection with services or treatment. **Private companies have no general AI disclosure obligation** — what reaches them is a list of prohibitions, every one of which requires intent. Enforcement is exclusively the Attorney General's, after a 60-day cure period.

If you searched "texas ai law" expecting a rule that makes every business label its AI, that rule does not exist. Guides telling every Texas company to add an AI disclosure to its website are describing a law that was never passed.

---

## Who Texas AI law binds, and how

| You are… | What TRAIGA does to you |
|---|---|
| A **government entity** using AI to interact with consumers | Disclose — clear, conspicuous, plain language — **before or at the moment of interaction** |
| A **health care provider** using AI in services or treatment | Disclose to the recipient or their personal representative **by the date service is first provided** (in emergencies, as soon as reasonably possible) |
| A **private company** | No disclosure duty. You face the prohibitions instead |
| An **insurer or federally insured financial institution** | Exempt, provided you comply with your existing regulatory regime |
| A **city trying to pass its own AI ordinance** | Preempted — TRAIGA is the single statewide baseline |

Note also who counts as a "consumer": a Texas resident acting in an **individual or household context**. An employer's AI-driven HR process is not a consumer interaction under TRAIGA.

---

## The prohibitions that do reach private companies

Six prohibitions apply to "any person," and this is where Texas private-sector exposure actually lives:

- intentionally manipulating behavior to incite self-harm, harm to others, or criminal activity,
- social scoring by government,
- biometric capture without consent (government),
- impairing US or Texas constitutional rights,
- **unlawful discrimination against a protected class — but intent must be shown; disparate impact alone is expressly not enough**,
- CSAM, unlawful deepfakes, and explicit conversations impersonating minors.

The intent requirement is the deliberate design choice that separates Texas from Colorado, and it is why TRAIGA passed while broader bills died. A model that produces a discriminatory *outcome* is not, by itself, a TRAIGA violation.

## Penalties

| Violation type | Civil penalty |
|---|---|
| Curable, or breach of a statement submitted to the AG | **$10,000 – $12,000** per violation |
| Uncurable | **$80,000 – $200,000** per violation |
| Continued violation | **$2,000 – $40,000 per day** |

Enforcement is exclusively the **Attorney General's**, who must give **60 days' notice and an opportunity to cure** first. There is **no private right of action**. Two dates worth tracking: the AG's online consumer complaint portal (the intake channel that will generate the first enforcement matters) was required to be live by **September 1, 2026**, and TRAIGA also created the Texas Artificial Intelligence Council and a regulatory sandbox.

## What Texas AI law does *not* do

Worth checking against your compliance checklist, because several vendors now sell "TRAIGA disclosure" modules for a duty that does not exist:

- **No duty for ordinary businesses to disclose AI use** to their customers. → The states that do impose broad disclosure duties: [Maine](/maine/chatbot-disclosure/) (chatbots) and [Colorado](/colorado/) (decision tools, from 2027).
- **No chatbot-specific rule.** → [Maine §1500-DD](/maine/chatbot-disclosure/) is the strictest in the country.
- **No pre-decision notice for AI-assisted employment decisions.** → [Illinois HB 3773](/illinois/hb-3773/) imposes exactly that, in force now.
- **No advertising disclosure rule for AI-generated humans.** → [New York GBL §296-b](/new-york/synthetic-performer/) covers that, in force since June 9, 2026.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does TRAIGA require private companies to disclose AI use?**
No. The disclosure duty applies to government entities interacting with consumers and to health care providers. Private companies are subject to the prohibitions, not to a disclosure mandate.

**When did the Texas AI law take effect?**
January 1, 2026. TRAIGA (HB 149) was signed June 22, 2025.

**What is the maximum penalty under Texas AI law?**
$200,000 per uncurable violation, with continuing violations at $2,000 to $40,000 per day, enforced by the Attorney General after a 60-day cure period.

**Can consumers sue under TRAIGA?**
No. Enforcement is exclusively the Attorney General's.

**Is disparate impact enough to violate TRAIGA?**
No. The act expressly states that disparate impact alone is not sufficient — intent to discriminate must be shown.

**Does Texas AI law override city ordinances?**
Yes. TRAIGA preempts local government AI regulation, making it the single statewide baseline.
