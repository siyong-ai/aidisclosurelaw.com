# Texas AI Law (TRAIGA, HB 149): Who Actually Has to Disclose AI

> **Short answer.** TRAIGA took effect **January 1, 2026**. The affirmative **disclosure** duties fall on two groups only: **government entities** using AI to interact with consumers, and **health care providers** using AI in connection with services or treatment. Private companies have **no general AI disclosure obligation** under TRAIGA. What reaches private companies is the prohibition list — behavioral manipulation, unlawful discrimination, CSAM and unlawful deepfakes, and infringement of constitutional rights.

This distinction is the single most common error in TRAIGA coverage. Guides that tell every Texas business to add an "AI disclosure" to its website are describing a law that does not exist.

---

## The disclosure duty, precisely

### Government entities

An agency that makes available an AI system intended to interact with consumers must disclose, **before or at the time of the interaction**, that the consumer is interacting with an AI system.

The disclosure must be **clear and conspicuous** and written in **plain language**. Not buried in a privacy policy, not a footnote in terms of service — at the point of interaction.

### Health care providers

A provider using AI in relation to health care services or treatment must give a clear and conspicuous disclosure to the recipient, or the recipient's personal representative, **not later than the date the service or treatment is first provided**.

**Emergency exception:** where the situation is an emergency, the disclosure is due as soon as reasonably possible.

### Who is not covered by the disclosure duty

Private businesses, unless they are a government entity or a health care provider in the sense the act uses. Note also that "consumer" is defined as a Texas resident acting in an **individual or household context** — it expressly excludes individuals acting in a commercial or employment context. So an employer's AI-driven HR process is not a "consumer" interaction under this definition.

## The prohibitions, which do reach private companies

| Prohibition | Who it binds | Intent required? |
|---|---|---|
| Manipulating behavior to incite self-harm, harm to others, or criminal activity | Any person | **Yes** — "intentionally aims to" |
| Social scoring by government | Governmental entity | **Yes** |
| Biometric capture without consent, infringing individual rights | Governmental entity | — |
| Impairing US or Texas constitutional rights | Any person | **Yes** — "sole intent" |
| Unlawful discrimination against a protected class | Any person | **Yes** — disparate impact alone is **not** sufficient |
| CSAM, unlawful deepfakes, explicit conversations impersonating minors | Any person | **Yes** — "with the intent of" |

The intent requirement is the deliberate design choice that separates Texas from Colorado. A model that produces a discriminatory *outcome* is not, by itself, a TRAIGA violation. That is a much narrower law than most summaries suggest — and it is why TRAIGA passed while broader bills did not.

**Exemptions:** certain insurance entities and federally insured financial institutions are exempt, provided they comply with or are regulated by other applicable law.

## Penalties

| Violation type | Civil penalty |
|---|---|
| Curable, or breach of a statement submitted to the AG | **$10,000 – $12,000** per violation |
| Uncurable | **$80,000 – $200,000** per violation |
| Continued violation | **$2,000 – $40,000 per day** the violation continues |

Enforcement is exclusively the Attorney General's, who may issue civil investigative demands and seek injunctive relief, attorney's fees, court costs, and investigative expenses. The AG must give **60 days' notice and an opportunity to cure** before enforcement.

**No private right of action.**

---

## Two things people miss

**The AG complaint portal.** TRAIGA required the Attorney General to post an online consumer complaint mechanism by **September 1, 2026**. That is the intake channel that will generate the first enforcement matters. If your organization has a Texas consumer-facing AI product, watch what gets filed there.

**Local ordinances are preempted.** Texas preempts local government AI ordinances. Companies should treat TRAIGA as a single statewide baseline rather than a patchwork of city rules — one of the few places in US AI law where you get uniformity.

**Plus:** TRAIGA creates a regulatory sandbox and the Texas Artificial Intelligence Council, and requires state agencies to report on their AI use through the Sunset Advisory Commission and the Department of Information Resources.

## Checklist

**If you are a government entity:**
- [ ] Identify every consumer-facing system that could be characterized as AI
- [ ] Put a clear, plain-language disclosure at the point of interaction — before or at the moment it starts
- [ ] Verify the disclosure is visible on the first screen, not in a linked policy
- [ ] Confirm no covered system escapes because it is "just a rules-based assistant"

**If you are a health care provider:**
- [ ] Map every AI use that touches services or treatment
- [ ] Attach the disclosure to the intake workflow so it fires on the first date of service
- [ ] Write the emergency path — the disclosure still has to happen, just later
- [ ] Train staff on who delivers the disclosure and how it is documented

**If you are a private company:**
- [ ] Stop writing a TRAIGA disclosure duty into your compliance plan — the duty is not there
- [ ] Audit for the prohibitions instead, starting with intent evidence: what did your design documents and marketing materials say the system was for?
- [ ] Check whether you are an exempt insurer or federally insured financial institution, and document the basis
- [ ] Prepare for a 60-day cure demand, since that is how enforcement will start

## Frequently asked questions

**Does TRAIGA require private companies to disclose AI use?**
No. The disclosure duty applies to government entities interacting with consumers and to health care providers. Private companies are subject to the prohibitions, not to a disclosure mandate.

**When did TRAIGA take effect?**
January 1, 2026. It was signed June 22, 2025.

**Is disparate impact enough to violate TRAIGA?**
No. The act expressly states that disparate impact alone is not sufficient to show intent to discriminate. Intent must be shown.

**What is the maximum penalty?**
$200,000 per uncurable violation, with continuing violations at $2,000 to $40,000 per day.

**Can consumers sue under TRAIGA?**
No. Enforcement is exclusively the Attorney General's, with a 60-day cure period.

**Does TRAIGA override city AI ordinances?**
Yes. Texas preempts local government AI regulation, so TRAIGA is the statewide baseline.
